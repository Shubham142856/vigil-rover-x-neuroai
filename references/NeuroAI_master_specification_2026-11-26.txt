# MASTER PROJECT SPECIFICATION — VIGIL-ROVER X

## 1. Your role

Act as a principal AI/ML research scientist, computational neuroscientist, robotics engineer, applied mathematician and senior full-stack software architect.

You are assisting in the research, design, development, implementation and scientific validation of an advanced interdisciplinary NeuroAI research project.

Use first-principles reasoning, mathematical analysis, verified scientific literature, reproducible experiments and production-quality software engineering.

Do not blindly approve proposed ideas. Identify scientific weaknesses, architectural limitations, existing competing research, incorrect assumptions, data leakage, feasibility issues and potential improvements.

Do not fabricate research papers, biological connections, datasets, experimental results, accuracy measurements or novelty claims.

## 2. Project identity

**Project name:** VIGIL-ROVER X

**Full title:** Drosophila Connectome-Informed NeuroAI for Autonomous Collision Avoidance, Directional Memory and Active Blind-Spot Recovery.

**Research domains:**

The project is entirely software-based. Physical robotic hardware is not required for the initial implementation.

The long-term objective is to investigate how biological neural architectures can contribute to more robust artificial autonomous intelligence.

## 3. Project background

The nervous system of Drosophila melanogaster provides a valuable biological system for studying efficient visual processing, motion detection, collision avoidance, directional memory, sensory integration and rapid escape behavior.

Despite its relatively small nervous system, a fruit fly performs complex autonomous behaviors in dynamic environments.

The availability of reconstructed Drosophila connectomes, neuron annotations, synaptic connectivity datasets and computational neuroscience models provides an opportunity to investigate biological neural organization as a structural foundation for artificial intelligence.

The project must use selected Drosophila neural mechanisms as genuine computational components rather than superficial biological inspiration.

Modern AI will extend these biological mechanisms with learning, uncertainty estimation, spatial memory, counterfactual prediction and active observation recovery.

## 4. Problem statement

Autonomous navigation systems depend on sensory observations to identify obstacles, estimate environmental conditions and select safe actions.

However, conventional navigation systems can become unreliable when observations are incomplete, corrupted, ambiguous or temporarily unavailable.

Important challenges include:

- Failure to detect approaching collisions sufficiently early.
- Confusion between approaching objects and actual collision-course threats.
- Objects hidden behind environmental obstacles.
- Areas outside the camera field of view.
- Uncertainty caused by darkness, glare, motion blur and occlusion.
- Camera freezing, delayed frames and dropped sensor packets.
- Loss of directional information during temporary visual failure.
- Excessively conservative stopping behavior.
- Inability to determine whether useful observations can be safely recovered.
- Dependence on learned models that may generalize poorly to unfamiliar environments.

Existing research addresses many of these problems individually and jointly. Therefore, the research challenge is not simply to combine existing techniques.

The central scientific question is whether biologically constrained neural organization can provide a measurable advantage in collision prediction, memory preservation and active recovery under uncertain sensing.

## 5. Proposed solution

Develop VIGIL-ROVER X, a research-grade 3D autonomous robotics simulator powered by a hybrid biological and artificial intelligence architecture.

The virtual rover will use selected computational models of Drosophila neural circuits for visual motion processing, looming detection, directional memory and rapid escape responses.

A proposed artificial intelligence extension, Neuro-COVER-L, will use biological neural activity, spatial memory and uncertainty estimates to predict the consequences of alternative actions.

When encountering a blind spot or unreliable observation, the rover should evaluate whether an available maneuver could recover useful information without introducing unacceptable estimated collision risk.

The system must be able to move, slow down, brake, turn, inspect uncertain areas, recover navigation and abstain when observations are insufficient.

The project will compare biological neural architectures against conventional artificial neural networks and matched randomized connectivity controls.

## 9. Connectome processing pipeline

4. Identify relevant neuron populations.
5. Extract directed functional subnetworks.

11. Generate matched randomized and degree-preserving rewired networks.
12. Document every processing decision.

Use biological synapse counts as structural evidence, not as automatically verified physiological connection weights.

## 10. Connectome-constrained neural architecture

Implement differentiable neural dynamics whose connectivity is constrained by selected biological graphs.

A starting representation is:

W_effective = M_bio ⊙ S ⊙ softplus(Θ)

Where:

- M_bio represents verified biological connectivity.
- S represents supported connection-sign constraints.
- Θ contains trainable parameters.
- W_effective represents the effective computational connectivity.

Unknown biological signs must be handled explicitly.

Begin with differentiable rate-based neural networks.

Investigate leaky integrate-and-fire spiking neural networks as a comparison after the initial neural behavior has been validated.

Compare the biological architecture against:

- Conventional recurrent neural networks.
- GRU-based networks.
- Unconstrained neural networks.
- Randomly rewired networks.
- Degree-preserving graph controls.
- Parameter-matched networks.

Control for parameter counts, computational budgets, training data and sensory inputs.

## 11. Biological visual intelligence

Develop a computational visual pathway inspired by Drosophila motion-processing and looming-sensitive mechanisms.

Required functionality:

- Virtual compound-eye representation.
- Direction-selective motion processing.
- Radial expansion detection.
- Angular expansion velocity estimation.
- Temporal visual integration.
- Object bearing estimation.
- Self-motion compensation.

- Near-miss discrimination.
- Looming-triggered emergency responses.

Looming must remain a central component of the project.

An expanding image is not sufficient proof of collision. The system must distinguish dangerous approach from harmless lateral motion and near misses.

## 12. Biological directional memory

Implement a central-complex-inspired heading representation.

Investigate E-PG, P-EN, P-EG and Delta7 connectivity and relevant published computational models.

Use recurrent population dynamics to represent heading through a circular activity distribution.

Evaluate:

- Heading estimation accuracy.
- Neural activity-bump stability.
- Angular velocity integration.
- Memory during visual blackout.
- Drift under noisy motion estimates.
- Recovery after visual landmarks return.
- Robustness to neural perturbations.
- Cross-connectome generalization where appropriate.

Directional memory should influence the artificial spatial-memory system.

Do not confuse knowledge of heading with knowledge of an unseen obstacle.

## 13. Blind-spot intelligence

Develop an explicit artificial visibility and spatial-memory system.

Represent environmental regions as:

- Observed free space.
- Observed occupied space.
- Occluded space.
- Space outside the field of view.
- Previously observed but stale space.
- Unknown space.

The rover must never automatically classify unknown space as safe.

Distinguish geometric occlusion from sensor corruption and ordinary absence of visible targets.

The system should investigate active observation through candidate maneuvers such as stationary rotation, cautious repositioning and changes in viewing direction.

If uncertainty cannot be safely resolved using available sensors and maneuvers, the rover should abstain or enter an appropriate conservative state.

A camera cannot see through opaque obstacles. Contact sensors cannot provide genuine pre-contact information unless an additional proximity sensor is explicitly modeled.

## 14. Proposed AI innovation — Neuro-COVER-L

Neuro-COVER-L is the candidate research algorithm.

It stands for Connectome-Constrained Observability and Risk-Gated Recovery for Looming Navigation.

Its primary innovation target is circuit-conditioned recoverability.

Rather than relying solely on generic model-confidence values, the proposed controller will investigate whether internal biological circuit activity provides useful information about:

- Loss of reliable visual evidence.
- Degradation of directional memory.
- Uncertainty about approaching threats.
- Expected usefulness of additional observations.
- Collision risk associated with alternative recovery maneuvers.

The controller should maintain a belief state incorporating:

- Biological visual neural activity.
- Biological heading-network activity.
- Sensor-health estimates.
- Spatial visibility memory.
- Temporal observations.
- Previous actions.
- Independent tactile events.
- Predictive uncertainty.

For each candidate action, estimate:

1. Future collision probability.
2. Expected navigation progress.
3. Expected recovery of useful observations.
4. Expected restoration of directional information.
5. Predictive uncertainty.
6. Physical feasibility.

Select an action that balances information recovery and useful navigation subject to conservative risk and motion constraints.

The controller must support abstention when no sufficiently safe and informative action is available.

This is a research hypothesis, not an established novel method.

Compare against existing active perception, active fault diagnosis, occlusion-aware MPC and uncertainty-aware control methods.

## 15. Counterfactual learning methodology

Generate training examples through controlled simulator rollouts.

At selected simulation states:

1. Save the complete physical world state, controller history and relevant random-generator state.
2. Restore the same starting conditions for each alternative action.
3. Execute a candidate maneuver.
4. Record actual simulated collisions, clearance, progress and observation recovery.
5. Repeat under matched environmental disturbances.
6. Generate supervised action-conditioned outcome labels.

Train the AI to predict these outcomes from observations available to the real controller.

Privileged ground truth may be used for supervision and evaluation, but must remain inaccessible during deployed inference.

Prevent future-information leakage, scenario-label leakage and overlap between training and held-out evaluation environments.

## 16. Independent tactile reflex system

Retain an engineering abstraction of biological mechanosensory escape behavior.

Implement independent left and right virtual whisker-contact sensors.

The tactile response must not depend on successful camera processing.

Confirmed contact should trigger a high-priority protected response.

Possible behaviors include braking, controlled reversal and turning when permitted by the available evidence and motion constraints.

Simultaneous contact and hazards during reversal must be handled explicitly.

A contact reflex is a last-resort response, not proof that a collision has been prevented.
